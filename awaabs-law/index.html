Awaab's Law in plain English

What social landlords in England must do, and by when.

Awaab's Law is named after Awaab Ishak, a two-year-old boy who died in December 2020 after long-term exposure to mould in his family's social housing flat in Rochdale.

It requires social landlords (councils and housing associations) to investigate and fix serious hazards within fixed legal timescales. It came into force on 27 October 2025 for damp, mould and emergency hazards, and is expanding in phases.

The clock starts when the landlord becomes aware of a potential hazard. That includes reports from tenants, contractors and staff on routine visits, not just calls to the contact centre.

If a case is challenged, the main defence is showing the landlord used all reasonable endeavours, which depends on clear, detailed records.

Seven hazards added from 30 November 2026

  • Electrical hazards
  • Fire and explosions
  • Excess cold
  • Excess heat
  • Falls, including on stairs and between levels
  • Structural collapse
  • Domestic hygiene, including food safety
